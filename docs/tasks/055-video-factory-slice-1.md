## 2. Checkpoints

Each checkpoint is a separate small PR that must be green on CI before the next starts. Test rows are numbered T1, T2, … across the whole slice. Each test row states what to prove and its negative control (the case that must fail). The Sol test writer writes these tests; each has one negative control (tasks 2.11 of `video-factory-v1`).

### Checkpoint 1 — schema, worker, sweep, fake runner, safe takeover, relauncher

Owned paths: `migrations/0060_*.sql`; `src/modules/flow/video-service.ts`, `video-jobs.ts`, `video-worker.ts`; `src/api/executor-lock.ts`; `src/api/operator-app.ts` (startup and wiring); `src/platform/video-process-runner.ts` and the other copied helpers; `deploy/systemd/user/tgos-operator.service`; `docs/runbooks/fedora-local-operator-runtime.md`; `src/api/executor-lock.ts` comment update. The shared lock and restart change may land as its own small PR inside 055 (054 §7 sequencing note).

Closes rows: 055-Q2, 055-Q3, 055-Q7, 055-Q18, 055-Q19.

Test rows:

- **T1** — Startup sweep: a new executor start marks a video job left `running` by a dead process as `interrupted`, with reason restart, before the server accepts requests. Negative control: a job already `succeeded` or `failed` is untouched by the sweep, and an interrupted job is never re-queued.
- **T2** — Two starters race for a stale lock after a proven-dead owner: exactly one wins; the loser exits with the held-lock error and never writes. Negative control: with the owner process still alive, neither starter may take the lock.
- **T3** — A lock from another host, and a lock with unreadable or corrupt metadata, keep the manual procedure: startup fails with the existing held-lock error and the lock file is unchanged. Negative control: a same-host, valid, proven-dead lock is taken by the takeover (the positive path must work).
- **T4** — PID reuse: a lock whose recorded PID now belongs to a different live process (different process start time or boot id) is treated as not proven dead and stays manual. Negative control: a matching start time and boot id with a dead PID passes the proof.
- **T5** — kill -9 the app during a video job using the fake runner: after restart the app serves again with no manual step, the job shows interrupted, and the takeover left the lock consistent for exactly one executor. Negative control: the interrupted job is not retried by the restart (no automatic retry, 054 §3).
- **T6** — A video job's outside call exceeds its time limit: only that job fails with a typed error; the app and other jobs keep running. Negative control: the timed-out call is not retried and no other provider or account is called (R-V11).
- **T7** — Worker injection: the worker calls the injected process runner, never a real ffmpeg, in unit tests. Negative control: a runner that reports a crash marks only its own job failed and leaves the app serving.
