Create a premium corporate B2B infographic poster. It must feel like a professional agency product, not a generic template. The viewer must understand the main topic in 3–5 seconds. Prioritize mobile readability, strong visual hierarchy, structured information, consistent spacing, clear modules, professional credibility, and repeatable brand identity.

### Content processing

Use the Caption reference data below as the sole editorial source. Identify the most suitable poster type: Education, Service, Q&A, Case Study, comparison, or process. Determine headline, hook, key message, important points, conclusion, CTA, and relevant brand information. Preserve meaning. Do not invent facts, numbers, legal claims, benefits, or services.

Shorten and group content into one primary structure: cards, numbered points, checklists, comparison blocks, Q&A blocks, timelines, or process steps. For long content prioritize headline, hook, four to six key ideas, conclusion, CTA, and footer; remove repetition and compress explanation. For short content improve structure and use whitespace rather than unsupported filler. Avoid overcrowding, long paragraphs, tiny text, and mixed content patterns.

### Design system

Use a corporate, modern, premium, clean, trustworthy editorial infographic style for B2B contexts such as legal, accounting, tax, business registration, virtual office, banking, real estate, or corporate consulting. Use Navy Blue `#082B61`, Deep Blue `#103E78`, Gold/Orange `#F2A900`, White `#FFFFFF`, Light Gray `#F5F7FA`, Border Gray `#D9E1EA`, and Dark Text `#17243A` consistently; use no more than four major colors. Use red only for warnings, mistakes, risks, or incorrect actions.

Avoid excessive effects, complex gradients, decorative backgrounds, cartoon illustrations, playful stock templates, posing models, mixed icon styles, dense text blocks, and excessive shadows. Use a Vietnamese-compatible modern sans-serif system: bold condensed hero headline, bold section headings, semibold card titles, readable regular body, and compact medium footer. All Vietnamese diacritics must be correct.

Use one same-family outline icon system with medium stroke and rounded ends, in navy, white, or gold. Suitable concepts include document, search, location, checklist, calculator, shield, lightbulb, clock, target, handshake, building, legal scale, tax file, support, and chart. Never combine outline icons with 3D or cartoon icons.

### Modular UX structure

Use a light header for logo, brand name/tagline, and inferred category. Make the hero dominant: concise two-to-four-line headline, restrained gold highlight, short hook, and at most one tightly cropped relevant realistic business image that never overpowers text. Do not use posing models or cartoon people.

Choose one primary main-content structure. Use four to six numbered rows for education; pain point/solution/benefit/USP or three to five benefit cards for service; three to five separated blocks for Q&A; problem/solution/result/key lesson for case study; two columns for comparison; or a four-to-six-step timeline for process content. Use white cards, light-gray borders, rounded corners, generous padding, thin separators, minimal shadows, navy headings, gold accents, and consistent icons. Keep titles short and descriptions to one to three readable lines.

Close with a concise conclusion and CTA appropriate to reference data. Educational content may invite following, saving, consultation, or further knowledge; service content may invite contact, consultation, quotation, or registration. Do not make the CTA more aggressive than the source. Finish with a concise navy footer distributing only available website, page, hotline, and address information with outline icons.

### Quality gate

Ensure professional balance, correct spelling, readable hierarchy, realistic relevant imagery, consistent icons, precise margins, aligned cards, no warped text/documents, no duplicate content, no irrelevant objects, no overcrowding, and no generic-template appearance. Reading order is brand/category, headline, hook, image, modules, conclusion, CTA, then footer. Emphasize headline first, key ideas second, conclusion third, and CTA fourth.
